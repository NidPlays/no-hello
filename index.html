Chat etiquette · est. 2013

Don't just say
“hello”.

Say hello and the question, in the same message. One keystroke's difference for you; minutes back for whoever you pinged.

K Keith Chat · Do not disturb

    What the “hello” actually sends

    • A notification, a badge, a sound, and a taskbar flash.
    • Zero information about what's needed.
    • An obligation to sit still and wait for part two.
    • A thread that is useless if either of you walks away.

    It's the chat equivalent of phoning someone, saying “hi!”, and putting them on hold. 🤦

    The whole argument

    Same request. Same politeness. Four minutes apart.

    Don't do this4m 51s
    1. Keith 2:15 PM

      hi

    2. Tim 2:19 PM

      ...?

    3. Keith 2:20 PM

      what time was taht thing again?

    4. Tim 2:20 PM

      oh — 3:30 mate

    Keith could have had his answer minutes sooner — and Tim spent four of those minutes wondering what was coming.

    Try this instead0m 41s
    1. Dawn 2:15 PM

      Hiya! What time was that thing?

    2. Tim 2:15 PM

      hey, 3:30

    3. Dawn 2:15 PM

      Ta — seeya then!

    4. Tim 2:16 PM

      👌 np

    Dawn said hello too. She just didn't stop there.

    Counts as “hello”:

    • “you around?”
    • “yt?”
    • “ping”
    • “got a sec?”
    • “quick question…”
    • “hi Sophie —”
    • “can I ask you something?”
    • “u there”

    Why this matters more in chat than in person

    Chat is not a phone call. That's the feature.

    01

    Typing is slower than talking

    In person, the pause between “hi” and your question is a second. In chat it's however long you take to compose it — while they sit there watching “typing…”.

    02

    Async only works if there's content

    A question left in a thread can be answered at 7am, from a phone, by someone else entirely. A lone “Hello” can only be answered by you, later, in person.

    03

    A notification is a bill

    Every ping spends a bit of someone's attention. Make it buy something. Two pings for one answer is twice the price for the same goods.

    04

    Future-you is searching this

    “Hello / hi / hey” is unsearchable. “What's the staging DB password rotation policy?” is the thing you'll be grepping your own history for in eight months.

    The common objection

    “But firing off a question feels rude.”

    Then don't fire off a question — send pleasantries and the question. Nobody is asking you to be curt. You can be as warm as you like, as long as the warmth and the ask arrive together.

    • ✓ “hey man, what's up? also, any idea when that thing's due?”
    • ✓ “Hi there! Hope you're well. I'm after the latest deck, whenever you get a sec :)”
    • ✓ “Morning! No rush at all — could you update those NFRs today?”
    • ✓ “Hiya 👋 sorry to interrupt — does the Friday demo need a dry run?”

    Notice that every one of them still says hello. Hello isn't the problem. Hello alone is.

    Please read this bit

    Seriously — don't get mad.

    This is only half serious. Maybe less.

    It's a chat convention, not a moral failing. If somebody sent you this link, they were being cheeky, not filing a complaint about you as a person. Please don't get mad at them.

    And don't be smug about it either.

    Linking this at a colleague mid-conversation is its own kind of rude. It's a gentle nudge for people who'd genuinely like to know — not a citation to win an argument with.

    Norms differ. Yours might be fine.

    Plenty of workplaces and cultures open with a greeting on purpose, and plenty of people are typing on their second or third language. Read the room you're actually in; this site isn't the boss of it.

    One gentle heads-up.

    If you spot this site's URL in someone's status, bio or signature, they've quietly told you how they prefer to be messaged. A lone “Hello!” might just sit there a while — not out of rudeness; they're only waiting for the rest of it.
